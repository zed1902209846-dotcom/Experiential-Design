# AR Solar System — Video Pitch Script

Total length: 2:55. Timecodes refer to AR-Solar-System-Video-Pitch-FULL.mp4.

## [0:00] Slide 1 — Cover

Hello, this is AR Solar System: an Android AR app for museums. Visitors scan printed planet cards, make a guess, and watch the planets prove it.

## [0:11] Slide 2 — Design Background

The project is designed for a table in the planet gallery of an astronomy museum, where visitors scan printed cards with a tablet the museum provides. Today, labels show numbers like three point seven metres per second squared. Visitors can read them, but they can't test them. Our goal is to answer three questions: why you jump higher on Mars, what sets a day and a year, and how different the planets are in size. AR cards suit this space. The comparison is physical and shared, the Sun card is a light you can move, and the museum only needs to print cards. Because visitors stand and stay briefly, we use short prompts, no sign-in, and a screen that stays awake.

## [0:51] Slide 3 — User Scenario

Our visitor is a child on a family visit, sharing one phone. The journey has six stages, from arriving to the reveal, and excitement peaks when the answer appears. Every interaction starts from a card or a fingertip: tap, drag, pinch, add a second card, or add the Sun card. We also tested the guess-first loop on paper, as a Wizard of Oz test, with a person acting as the app. Here is the prototype in action.

## [1:21] Demo video

- **1:21** The visitor opens the app and taps scan.
- **1:25** Pointing the camera at the Earth card brings the planet onto the table.
- **1:33** A pinch resizes it, and a drag spins it, so visitors can look from any side.
- **1:43** Now the Sun card joins. As the visitor moves it, Earth's lit half turns to face it, showing day and night.
- **1:57** Adding a second card, Saturn, opens the lab with four experiments: jump, day, year, and size.
- **2:05** First: who jumps higher? The visitor guesses Earth, and taps jump.
- **2:13** Saturn wins, by four centimetres! Its gravity is close to Earth's.
- **2:18** Next: whose year is shorter? Tap orbit,
- **2:21** and both planets circle the Sun card. Earth laps Saturn again and again.
